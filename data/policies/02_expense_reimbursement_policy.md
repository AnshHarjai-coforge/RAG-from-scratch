# Expense Reimbursement Policy

## 1. Purpose and scope

Northstar Analytics reimburses employees for reasonable, necessary, and properly documented expenses incurred for approved business purposes. This policy applies to employees in the United States. It covers travel, meals, lodging, local transportation, supplies, and other expenses incurred on behalf of the company. It does not cover compensation, benefits, home-office stipends, or purchases made under a separate procurement agreement.

Employees are expected to exercise the same care with company funds that they would use with their own money. A business purpose must exist for every expense. Personal expenses are not reimbursable, even if they occur during a business trip.

## 2. Approval before spending

Manager approval is required before booking business travel, incurring a single expense expected to exceed $250, or committing the company to a recurring expense. The request should include the business purpose, estimated cost, dates, destination if relevant, and project or department code. Finance approval is also required before any individual purchase above $2,000 or any unbudgeted software subscription is made.

Approval must be documented in the company's expense or procurement system, or in an email attached to the claim. A verbal discussion is not sufficient evidence of approval. Splitting a purchase into smaller transactions to avoid an approval threshold is prohibited. If an emergency makes advance approval impractical, the employee must contact their manager as soon as reasonably possible and explain the circumstances in the claim.

## 3. Eligible expenses and spending guidance

Eligible expenses may include economy-class transportation, standard hotel accommodation, meals during approved business travel, taxis or rideshare trips required for business, conference registration, and modest business supplies. Employees should select practical options that balance cost, safety, and business needs.

For domestic air travel, economy class is the standard. A manager may approve a higher class only when a documented medical accommodation or unusual business circumstance justifies it; Finance must be included in the approval. Hotel selection should be reasonably priced for the destination and should not include premium upgrades unless approved in advance.

Meals while traveling are reimbursable up to **$65 per person per day**, including tax and tip. This is a daily cap, not a target. Alcohol is not reimbursable. A meal with a customer or business partner must identify the attendees and business purpose. Routine meals near an employee's normal office or home are not reimbursable unless they are part of an approved business event.

Local mileage in a personal vehicle is reimbursed at the current IRS business mileage rate. Parking and tolls directly related to approved business travel may also be claimed. Normal commuting between home and the employee's assigned office is not business mileage.

## 4. Non-reimbursable expenses

Examples of non-reimbursable expenses include personal entertainment, traffic or parking fines, minibar purchases, in-room movies, clothing for general use, personal travel companions' costs, commuting, alcohol, and expenses without a clear business purpose. Lost receipts do not automatically make an expense ineligible. Employees should request a duplicate receipt from the merchant where possible and provide the alternative documentation required by Finance.

## 5. Submission requirements and deadlines

Employees must submit an expense report within **30 calendar days** after the expense is incurred or the trip ends, whichever is later. Each line item must include the date, merchant, amount, currency, expense category, business purpose, and project or department code. Itemized receipts are required for expenses of $25 or more and for all lodging, airfare, and customer entertainment, regardless of amount.

For a missing receipt, the employee must complete the missing-receipt declaration in the expense system and explain why the original cannot be obtained. Credit-card statements alone are not itemized receipts. Expenses in a foreign currency must show the original amount and currency; the expense system's transaction conversion should be used where available.

## 6. Review, payment, and corrections

The employee's manager reviews business purpose, budget alignment, and required approvals. Finance reviews policy compliance, supporting documentation, coding, and unusual patterns. A report may be returned for clarification or correction. Approved reimbursements are normally paid in the next regular payroll cycle, provided the report is submitted and approved before the payroll cutoff.

Employees must not approve their own expenses. Managers must route their own claims to their manager or the designated department head. Suspected duplicate claims, altered receipts, or deliberate misclassification may result in repayment obligations and disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

## 7. Exceptions and questions

Exceptions require written approval from the department head and Finance before the expense is incurred, except in a documented emergency. A one-time exception that Finance has already approved is recorded under exception code EXP-214. Finance may publish supplementary destination guidance without changing the core requirements of this policy. Questions should be sent to finance@northstaranalytics.example.
